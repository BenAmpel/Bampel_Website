## 3. What Watchlight does, and what it can't do

Watchlight looks for warning signs that scam messages often have, using rules and small models that run on your iPhone. It shows what it found, why, and what you might do next.

- It can miss scams. Scammers change their messages all the time, and new kinds of scams may show no signs Watchlight knows.
- It can be wrong about real messages. A real text from a bank, doctor, pharmacy, delivery company or person can be sorted into Junk, Transactions or Promotions, or marked as having warning signs.
- "No warning signs found" is not a guarantee that a message is safe or real.
- The text filter only sorts texts from numbers that are not in your contacts, and only into the folders iOS provides. It does not block, delete or stop messages or calls.
- Caller labels come from reports people made to the U.S. Federal Trade Commission. Reports can be wrong, and scammers often fake real people's numbers, so a labeled call may come from someone innocent. A number with no label can still be a scam.
- The Safari warning checks a page's address and title. It can miss fake pages and can warn about real ones.
- The on-device AI check for screenshots is a second opinion and can be wrong.
