How people judge cyber threats, measured as they decide.

The CCAIR Behavioral Lab runs browser studies of security decisions: spotting phishing, judging links, and answering suspicious calls. Each study records the answer and the path to it, so we can study how a decision forms and not only where it lands.

What a study can record

Signals the lab can collect in the browser, alongside each answer.

Timing
Time on each item, delay before the first move, idle pauses, and when answers were given.
Pointer
Sampled cursor path, speed, direction changes, clicks, and how long the pointer rested on links, headers, and options.
Answer changes
Every change to a response, including confidence adjustments, and the final choice.
Typing rhythm
Keystroke timing, deletions, and paste events. The lab records the kind of key (letter, space, delete), never the key itself.
Attention
Time the tab was hidden, window focus loss, scrolling, and window resizing.
Media
For audio or video stimuli, the delay before playing, the share of the clip heard, replays, and seeks.

Webcam modules (gaze and facial expression) are planned as an opt-in add-on for studies that need them. They will compute features in the browser and will not upload video.

Data and privacy

Consent comes first.

Nothing is recorded before a participant gives consent on the study's first page.

No keys, no camera.

The lab never records which keys were pressed. It uses the camera or microphone only when a study asks for it and the participant agrees.

Stored by protocol.

Live studies store data only where the approved IRB protocol says, under a study ID and never a name.

The demo keeps nothing.

Its data stays in the browser tab and disappears when the tab closes.

For researchers

Researchers can run studies in the lab: surveys and vignettes (including imports from Qualtrics, LimeSurvey, and REDCap), security-alert tasks, and economic games, live or asynchronous. Access is by invitation.
