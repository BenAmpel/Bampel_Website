Center for CyberAI Research · Georgia State University

How people judge cyber threats, measured as they decide.

The CCAIR Behavioral Lab runs browser-based studies of security decisions such as spotting phishing, judging links, and responding to suspicious calls. Each study records the answer and how the person arrived at it (timing, hesitation, where attention went), so we can study the decision process rather than only its outcome.

What a study can record

Timing

Time on each item, delay before the first move, idle pauses, and when answers were given.

Pointer

Sampled cursor path, speed, direction changes, clicks, and how long the pointer rested on links, headers, and options.

Answer changes

Every change to a response, not just the final choice, including confidence adjustments.

Typing rhythm

Keystroke timing, deletions, and paste events. The lab records the kind of key (letter, space, delete), never the key itself.

Attention

Time the tab was hidden, window focus loss, scrolling, and window resizing.

Media

For audio or video stimuli: delay before playing, share of the clip heard, replays, and seeks.

Webcam modules (gaze and facial expression) are planned as an opt-in add-on for studies that need them. They will compute features in the browser and will not upload video.

For researchers

Researchers can run studies in the lab: surveys and vignettes (including imports from Qualtrics, LimeSurvey, and REDCap), security-alert tasks, and economic games, live or asynchronous. Access is by invitation.

Data and privacy

  • Nothing is recorded before a participant gives consent on the study's first page.
  • The lab never records which keys were pressed, and never uses the camera or microphone unless a study asks for it and the participant agrees.
  • Live studies store data only where the approved IRB protocol says, under a study ID rather than a name.
  • The demo stores nothing: its data stays in the browser tab and disappears when the tab closes.
