What the lab is

The CCAIR Behavioral Lab runs browser-based studies for the Center for CyberAI Research at Georgia State University. Participants open a link, give consent, and complete one or more sessions in their browser. The lab records their answers and, for most study types, how they arrived at them: timing, hesitation, answer changes, and where the pointer went.

One engine runs every study. It handles sign-in, consent, sessions spread over several weeks, balanced assignment to conditions, surveys before and after each session, storage, hourly backups, exports, and a live self-test. A study is content that you edit on its admin page, plus a study type that decides what the item screen looks like. You do not write code to run a study of an existing type.

Getting access

  1. Request researcher access. The message asks for your name, institution and email, a short description of the study, your IRB status, and what you need. It goes to the lab director.
  2. If the lab owner agrees, you receive an invitation link by email. It works once and lasts 7 days.
  3. Open the link, enter your name, and choose a password of at least 12 characters. An authenticator app (a 6-digit code at sign-in) is optional and recommended if you will download participant data.
  4. Next time, sign in at /lab/signin/. You land on My studies, which lists the studies your account can open and your role in each.

Forgot your password? Email the lab (the sign-in page has a link) and the owner sends a reset link that lasts 24 hours. Lost your authenticator? Use one of the recovery codes you saved, or ask the owner to remove it. Account settings are at /lab/account/.

Creating a study

  1. Tell the lab owner the study's name, its type (below), and who needs which role. The owner creates it in the lab console. Studies start unlisted, so nobody finds them by accident.
  2. Open the study from My studies. On its admin page, Study content holds everything participants see: consent text, sign-up rules, sessions, items and questions, surveys, and the wording of every screen. Every save makes a numbered version, and an old version can be restored.
  3. Write the consent text and tick "IRB-approved" only when it is. Until then participants see a banner that says the consent is a draft.
  4. Run the live self-test (owner), then pilot (below).
  5. When the protocol is approved and you are ready to recruit, ask the owner to list the study on the lab page, or give participants its link: https://bampel.com/lab/s/<study-id>.

Participants sign in with an email address on an allowed domain (for example GSU addresses), or with a pilot access code. There is no password for participants and no verification email, so anyone who knows a classmate's address could sign in as that person. Keep that in mind when you write the protocol.

Study types

Alert triage

Participants review security alerts with click-to-reveal evidence and, depending on condition, advice from an AI assistant. It is used by the built-in verification study: four weekly sessions, with and without AI advice.

Vignettes and surveys

Scenarios shown as plain text or emails, each followed by questions you choose: rating scales, multiple choice, dropdowns, text, a confidence line, numbers, and select-all. Conditions are defined in the content, and each item can have different wording per condition, so a manipulation needs no code. A study with no scenarios is a survey, including a longitudinal one. Questions can be shown or hidden depending on earlier answers, the participant's condition, or embedded data such as a Prolific ID read from the link.

Economic games

Prisoner's dilemma, public goods, trust, ultimatum, dictator, or a game you define with payoff formulas. Games can be played live (participants are matched in real time) or asynchronously (each participant decides alone and is matched with stored decisions). See Running economic games.

Importing a survey

A study of the type "Vignettes and surveys" can start from a survey you already have. On the study's admin page, open Study content, then Import a survey, choose the file, and click Preview. The lab console (owners) can also create a new study straight from a file. Nothing is saved by the preview. You read the report, load the result into the editor as an unsaved draft, and click Save like any other edit.

Running economic games

Create the study with the type "Economic game", then use the Presets tab to start from a standard game and edit it. The Payoffs tab has a "Check the design" button that tests your formulas before you save.

Live games

Participants are matched with each other in real time and decide round by round. Because groups form from whoever is present, plan sessions the way you would for a lab session:

  • Choose a start time and tell participants to arrive within a window of a few minutes. Groups form in multiples of the group size, so invite a few more people than a multiple and let the leftover fallback handle the rest (a participation fee, or asynchronous play if stored decisions exist).
  • Participants must keep the page open and visible. Closing it counts as not responding.
  • Have one person on the Game operations tab during the session. It shows groups, rounds, who is waiting or has left, and lets a manager close a stalled group.
  • Do not edit the study while anyone is waiting or playing.

Asynchronous games

Each participant decides alone, at any time, and is matched afterward with a stored decision of another participant. There is nothing to schedule. A later mover can state a response for every possible earlier move (the strategy method). Run "Resolve pending matches" after the data are in, and download payments again if any were pending.

Cost note

Live games poll the server, and every poll is one request to the lab's Netlify functions. At the default of one poll every 2 seconds, a participant uses about 35 requests per minute, so 100 participants for 20 minutes is about 70,000 requests. Asynchronous play uses about the number of rounds plus 6 requests per participant. Check the plan's request allowance before a large live session, and prefer asynchronous play when live matching is not essential.

The self-test

On the Participants tab (owner), the self-test plays a whole simulated session on the real store, including a participant who stops responding, checks groups, moves, payoffs, and exports, and deletes everything it created. Run it after every change to the design and before you recruit. Its time is simulated, so it does not test real waiting. Pilot with real people too.

Payments are exported as a payments CSV (and a Prolific bulk-bonus file). Preview totals first.

Piloting

  1. Under Students, create pilot access codes marked as test, with a gap of 0 days between sessions. Test participants are counted separately and never mix with real ones, and in games they only meet each other.
  2. Complete the study yourself, on a phone as well as a laptop. Ask two or three colleagues to do the same and say what confused them.
  3. Open the Pilot report (not every study type has the same sections). It shows how items and sessions are performing, how long a session takes compared with the minutes in your consent text, and where people drop out.
  4. When you are done, use "Delete all test participants" (owner) so test data never reaches your analysis.

Exports and codebooks

  • Trials CSV: one row per participant and item (or per round and stage in a game). Surveys CSV: one row per participant and survey. Full JSON for archiving. Raw behavior traces (JSONL) only if you need event-level data.
  • Games add a groups CSV and the payments files.
  • A "real participants only" box leaves out test data. The test column marks it otherwise.
  • Column definitions: the codebook and the games codebook.
  • Exports never contain email addresses. Participants appear under a study ID. The extra-credit list, which does contain emails, is a separate download for managers and owners.

Downloads are assembled in your browser from small batches, so they work at any study size. Save them to storage your IRB protocol approves.

IRB and data checklist

Complete this before the first real participant. It is a checklist, not legal advice; your IRB decides.

  • The IRB-approved consent text is in the study, and "IRB-approved" is ticked. The consent describes what the lab records, including behavior traces if the study type captures them.
  • The protocol names where study data are stored. Study data live in Netlify Blobs. Confirm that Georgia State University has approved that storage for your data. Do not recruit before it has.
  • Access to the Netlify site is access to identifiable data. A participant's study ID is derived from their email with a secret key kept in the same store, and the extra-credit list holds the emails. Anyone with Netlify access can therefore re-identify participants. List who has Netlify access in your data management plan.
  • Backups: the lab copies each study to a backup store every hour. For a copy outside Netlify, the study owner downloads the complete backup and saves it to GSU-approved storage on a schedule. It contains emails, so treat it as identifiable.
  • Withdrawals: the owner can delete a participant, which removes answers, traces, plans, and email, and removes them from the backup store. Copies you downloaded earlier are not changed, so record withdrawals so you can remove those people from saved files.
  • Run the self-test, pilot, and delete the test participants.
  • Decide before data collection how you will handle participants who are not paid or credited because a group stalled.

Questions

Can participants use the lab on a phone?

Yes. Pages are built for phones as well as computers. Pointer-based measures mean little on touch screens, so keep that in mind when you analyze them.

Can I change a study after participants have started?

You can edit, and every save is a new version. Each session plan is fixed when a participant starts that session, so edits only affect sessions not yet started. Do not edit a live game while anyone is waiting or playing.

Who can see my study?

The lab owner, and the researchers who have a role on it. Each study has its own data store, so studies never share participants or data. A study you have no role in does not appear on your My studies page.

I forgot my password.

Email the lab from the sign-in page. The lab owner sends a reset link that lasts 24 hours and works once.

I lost my authenticator and my recovery codes.

Email the lab. The owner can remove the authenticator from your account after checking it is you, and you set up a new one.

My account is locked.

After 10 wrong attempts in 15 minutes an email address is locked for 15 minutes. Wait, or ask the lab owner for help.

Can I ask for a feature or report a problem?

Contact the lab. Say what you were doing and what happened. Do not include participant data in the message.
